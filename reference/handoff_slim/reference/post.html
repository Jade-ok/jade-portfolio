Arrival, and Ted Chiang’s Story of Your Life

Villeneuve keeps the aliens at arm’s length so the real strangeness can happen inside a sentence.

The novella opens in a tense that English does not really have. Chiang writes toward a daughter who is at once unborn and already gone, and the effect is not a trick — it is the thesis.

The film has to solve this differently. Cinema is relentlessly present tense: whatever is on screen is happening now. So Villeneuve borrows the language of memory and lets us misfile the future as the past for two hours.

What survives the adaptation intact is the ethics. Louise is given a complete view and chooses the painful version anyway, not out of fatalism but because a life is not a problem to be optimized.
